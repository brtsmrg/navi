Navi: what it needs to be correct, legal and good

Navi is clean on the things that get most vibe-coded apps in trouble: no keys in the app, no ads, no tracking, no accounts, no known security holes. What is missing is the paper: no privacy policy, no terms, no credits screen, no contact address. Those block both app stores. This page lists every gap with its source, compares Navi with the map apps people already use, and gives the order to fix things.

1. What changed in the app today

Your phone report: every option said location was blocked, Free roam was a black screen, Fun loop and Near me did nothing, and the saved-roads buttons failed. The cause was one thing seen five ways. When the phone refuses location, each feature failed on its own with a tiny message and no way forward.

Fixed in the code, tested on a phone-sized screen
  • Location help card. It appears the moment the phone says no, on the home screen and in Free roam. On iPhone it gives the four Settings steps (Privacy & Security, Location Services, Safari Websites, aA > Website Settings), with a Try again button. Android and desktop have their own steps.
  • Start point without GPS. Search a place under "Start point". Fun loop, Near me, Game mode and saved roads then start from there. Free roam still needs live location, and says so.
  • Draw an area to save. A full-screen map. Pick circle or square, touch the middle, drag outward. It shows pieces, a rough size in MB and minutes before you save, and stops at 150 km across. Over 300 pieces it asks a second time and warns about load on the public servers.
  • Free roam screen. It now says "Waiting for your location" instead of "Clear road ahead", and the card scrolls so the Stop button is never pushed off.
  • Contrast. The red active tab and the "Change" link failed the 4.5 to 1 text rule. Fixed with a lighter red for text.
  • Honest wording. My first draft of the help card said location "stays on this phone". That is not true (see section 2), so it now says what is sent.

Checks run: types, lint, 173 tests (19 of them new), production build; Playwright with location denied and granted; axe on home, help card and picker. Not possible here: real iPhone Safari (no WebKit in this workspace). The iPhone menu names (Safari Websites, aA) come from Apple's and third-party how-tos and must be confirmed on your phone.

What Navi has that they do not (to confirm before claiming)

  • Plain-English calls by default, with the Numbers 1 to 6 style one tap away and explained.
  • Free roam that follows the road you are on without a destination, and a Fun loop that picks the twistiest round trip with no road driven twice.
  • No account, no ads, no tracking, and roads saved on the phone for use with no signal.
  • Game mode for practising calls while parked.

Price check: Navi's plan of US$49 a year is about double Kurviger's yearly plan range. Your launch-window lifetime price matters more than the yearly one. Worth a test before you commit.

5. The order to do it in

Done today, waiting to publish
  • Location help, start point, draw-an-area, contrast fixes, honest wording.
Phase 1: legal minimum, before anyone else uses it (about 2 days)
  • "About and legal" screen: what leaves the phone, safety disclaimer, map credits and licence link, open-source notices, AI-voice note, erase-everything button.
  • Privacy policy and terms as plain pages, drafted from section 2.
  • Error screen, 404, robots, share tags, npm audit in CI.
  • Stop sending no-referrer to Overpass.
  • Needs from you: a contact email, the legal name or "sole proprietor", and your province.
Phase 2: platform-ready (the backend plan, in this order)
  • Move to Cloudflare Pages so the written security headers apply.
  • Own road packs on cheap storage, built from OpenStreetMap, replacing Overpass for users. Credit and licence link built in.
  • Native wrapper with background location; store privacy forms; CarPlay request.
  • A Canadian privacy lawyer reads the policy once.
Phase 3: growth
  • Offline map picture, shared routes if you decide to add accounts, convoy rides, more languages.
